## Goal

The operator sees at once what waits for them and why, and answers it with the evidence and each
answer's consequence in front of them. They watch the agent that is working, and read every fact once,
in plain words. The page stays a thin view over the API it uses today.

- **A1 [ACTIVE]:** no change to the server's JSON API, the workflow, `client.py` or `stack.py`. Every
  datum the design shows is already in the API; the inventory is under Verified evidence.
- **A2 [ACTIVE]:** system font stacks, with no vendored web font. The CSP is `default-src 'self'`, and
  the page's one vendored dependency, xterm.js, is already the pinned-by-hand cost the interfaces'
  structure names.
- **A3 [ACTIVE]:** a run's own controls by its state: open and not stopping, *Stop run* then *Force

  two after; stopping, *Force terminate* alone; closed, neither. Force terminate's explanation stays in

- **A4 [ACTIVE]:** the New run form is the main area's content at `#new`, reached from *New run* in the

- **A5 [ACTIVE]:** the stack's reading stays at the top, one chip per part. Each part's and the whole
  stack's Start, Stop and Restart sit in a popover opened from the chips — two clicks, deliberately. A
  part the stack's owner reads `down` and `startable` raises a banner with what it blocks and its Start,
  the one one-click recovery. A part it cannot read, or cannot start from here, raises a banner with the
  owner's reason and no Start; one running or starting shows its state and offers nothing. The page
  offers only what the owner's `managed`, `startable` and `state` allow, as it does today (fact 32).
- **A6 [RESOLVED by D6]:** the working role's terminal is open and an idle role's is collapsed; either
  opens with a click, and stays as the operator left it across refreshes (Q1).
- **A7 [RESOLVED by D6]:** confirmations are the page's own `<dialog>`, with the same questions, a

- **A8 [ACTIVE]:** the selected run and view are in the URL's fragment (`#run=<id>`, `#new`,

- **A9 [ACTIVE]:** answers keep their names — Approve, Revise, Guide, Continue, Merge, Discard, Stop run,

- **A11 [ACTIVE]:** the design's subject is a console for conducting an ensemble of agents. Its audience

## Non-goals

- No change to what the workflow publishes, to what an answer means, or to which answers a stop offers.
  No change to the command line.
- No new endpoint or API field, no server-driven UI, no framework, no build step, no web font, no new
  vendored dependency.
- No light theme, no browser notifications, no push channel in place of polling, no new operator
  capability.
- No change to the flow files, such as a description field.

## Verified evidence

Captured by a throwaway instrument: headless Edge over the DevTools protocol, rendering the real page.
Every API answer came from fixtures that the real workflow produced on Temporal's time-skipping test
server with the suite's fake agents — a research brief revised then approved, a plan sent back once,
a blocker, a failed step, a spent round budget, the final gate, a merge — plus the at-work, stopping,
blocked and kept views built from those statuses. Nothing reached the server's API. Twelve states were

### Verified facts

**Defects**

1. **A closed run offers *Stop run* and *Force terminate*.** `.actions { display: flex }` in
   [style.css](../app/interfaces/workbench/static/style.css) outranks the `hidden` attribute:
   `#run-controls` measured `hidden = true`, `display: flex`, with both buttons visible on a stopped
   run. `#run-blocked` measured the same. The server refuses both presses.
2. **The research approval is titled "Approve the plan"**. `TITLES.approval` in
   [app.js](../app/interfaces/workbench/static/app.js) is one title for every approval, while the
   workflow's hint beside it says "approve to go on to the plan, or revise with feedback for new research".
3. **Before any plan exists, the page shows `todo: <path>` of the plan file.** `todo_path` is set when
   the worktree is made ([activities.py](../app/application/activities.py) `create_worktree`), and every
   stop publishes it ([workflow.py](../app/orchestration/workflow.py) `_stop`), including the research
   approval and a failed first plan.
4. **"blocked:" is glued to the elapsed time** in a run's row ("for 21:21blocked: the Windows worker is
   down"). `refreshRuns` appends the span with no separator.
5. **A failed run's log path overflows the rail.** Its row has no word breaking.
6. **Text cannot be selected reliably.** `refreshRun` rebuilds the whole history and the workflow lines
   every 2.5 s (`renderTimeline` calls `replaceChildren()`, and `lines.textContent` is reassigned), and
   `refreshRuns` rebuilds every row every 5 s. Also an inference: a text selection in them is cleared on
   the next refresh.

**Duplicates** (one fact, shown more than once)

7. A research brief is shown whole twice: in the stop card, as `stop.feedback`, and in the history, as
   `entry.brief` (workflow `_approval`, `_stage`).
8. The architect's feedback at an approval, a blocker or a spent budget is shown whole twice, in the
   stop card and in the history's last entry.
9. A failure is shown twice: its first line in the run's status line, and whole in the stop card.
10. "Waiting for you" is said three times — the row, the run's status line and the stop card's title.
    The elapsed time is shown three times.
11. The worktree path is repeated as the prefix of the plan path.

**Priority** (measured on the captures)

12. The stack panel is the first thing on the page: nine buttons, always visible while every part is
    up — *Start stack* among them, which then has nothing to start.
13. The Start form fills the top of the rail. "Needs you" begins 711 px down the empty page.
14. In a waiting run, *Stop run* and *Force terminate* come before the stop's answers.
15. Each terminal is about 715 px tall and shown whether live or idle. At the final gate the
    architect's verification and the change start about 2,270 and 2,360 px down, below both idle
    terminals.
16. The worktree tool, a maintenance view, sits in the rail at all times, with a second copy of the
    repository picker.

**Intuitiveness and hints**

17. Raw codes reach the operator: `approval`, `blocker`, `exhausted`, `final`, `MERGED`, `STOPPED`,
    "episode", "round 0", "phase research".
18. "for 12:28" can read as a time of day. It is minutes and seconds (`elapsed()`).
19. Four things are never explained on the page:
    - what *skip approvals* skips;
    - which of Repository and "or its path" wins — the path, silently;
    - which answers read "Your note";
    - how Stop run differs from Force terminate, until its confirmation.
20. A row shows about 45 characters of the task and nothing else that tells two runs apart, such as
    a start time or an id. With nothing selected, the main area is empty.
21. A refused answer ("revise needs the feedback to act on") shows below the buttons, not at the note,
    and focus stays where it was.

**Checklist baseline** — `web-design-review` on the three page files, 25 findings:
- no `color-scheme: dark`, so the checkbox and scrollbars render light;
- no skip link, no `aria-live` on the six result lines, no hover or keyboard access on the clickable
  `<li>` rows;
- hard-coded date and duration formats, no `tabular-nums` on the ticking times;
- unspecific labels ("Start", "Show", "Continue");
- no `name`, `autocomplete` or `spellcheck` on the fields, and placeholders that are not examples;
- the selected run is not in the URL;
- a smooth scroll that ignores reduced motion;
- no `translate="no"` on ids and paths.

**Consumers and constraints**

22. `tools/demo_press.py` reaches into the page in four ways:
    - the globals `select()` and `terminals[role]`;
    - the ids `start-task`, `start-repo`, `stop-note` and `start-result`;
    - the rule that a button's `-result` line is in its own `.card`;
    - `window.confirm`, whose question it records.

    `tools/demo.py` presses buttons by their labels. It checks the page's result words (`answered:`,
    `stopping`, `terminated`, `done: start WSL worker`, `not accepted`, `removed`, `started`) and the
    confirmations' words. [acceptance_restart.py](../tests/acceptance_restart.py) uses the API only.
23. [test_workbench.py](../tests/interfaces/test_workbench.py) proves the API and that the page's
    files are served; the page in a browser is `make demo`'s.
24. The server serves only the paths in its `STATIC` map.
25. The CSP `default-src 'self'` allows same-origin module scripts.
26. A terminal is fixed at 160×48, the size the host's PTY is made with (`terminal.py` `COLS, ROWS`), and
    the page's xterm must match it. A smaller xterm would garble a full-screen agent CLI, so an idle
    terminal can be collapsed or placed lower, never shrunk.
27. `review_diff` stages new files into a private index ([worktrees.py](../app/workspace/worktrees.py)),
    so at a plan's approval a run's change *is* its plan file. The existing change reader can show the plan.
28. **API inventory:** every datum the design shows is already served.
    - Each row: the run's view (`client.view`) — its state, stage, role, since, stop reason, failure,
      blocked_by, repo, host, flow, step and started time.
    - A run: the raw stop — id, reason, phase, todo, feedback, hint, actions and since.
    - The history: timeline entries — stage, phase, round, verdict, gate, feedback, brief and time.
    - The rest: the workflow's state (worktree, target), the run's links, the change in parts, the
      stack's reading, flows, repositories and worktrees.
    - A stop's phase is what tells a research approval from a plan's.
29. **xterm opens only in a visible parent.** Its API documentation for `open(parent)`: "This element
    must be visible (have dimensions) when `open` is called". An idle terminal cannot be opened inside
    a collapsed section and measured later.
30. **A build's approval is reachable.** `flows.check` lets `you:approve` follow any review or research,
    and asks only that `you:merge` come last, right after a verify ([flows.py](../app/foundation/flows.py)).
    A flow with two builds stops for approval after its first verify:
    `test_an_approval_says_where_it_goes_and_a_blocker_keeps_its_own_hint` drives one there, and its hint
    reads "approve to go on to the build, or revise with feedback for a new build". Neither shipped flow
    has such a step; a flow file may.
31. **The installed Edge is 154.** Probed in it: `Intl.DurationFormat` ("1 hr, 5 min"),
    `Intl.RelativeTimeFormat` ("4 hr. ago"), the `popover` attribute, `<dialog>.showModal()` and module
    scripts.
32. **Start is offered only for a part proven down.** The stack's owner marks a part `startable` only
    when its process was read and is not elevated (`stack.status`), and the page offers Start only for a
    part `down` and `startable` (`renderStack`, `renderBlocked`). A part that cannot be read is `unknown`,
    never startable.
33. **Opening a modal dialog dismisses an auto popover.** MDN, *Using the Popover API*:
    "`popover="auto"` popovers are also dismissed by successful `HTMLDialogElement.showModal()` … calls
    on other elements in the document."

## Current architecture and source of truth

- **The page** is architecture D29's: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript over the server's JSON API. It
  holds no state. Every write goes through `client.py` or the stack's owner (D32), and agent text
  reaches it only as text or terminal bytes.
- **Who owns what it shows:**
  - A run's facts are the workflow's status query, shaped by `client.view`.
  - The stack's reading is `stack.status`.
  - A change is `worktrees.review_diff`, read on the run's host.
  - The answers a stop takes, and whether one is accepted, are the workflow's (architecture D6). The
    page owns only their labels, colours and dialogs — the interfaces'
    [invariants](../app/interfaces/docs/architecture/structure.md#invariants) — and keeps no list of
    its own.
- **The page's code** is one classic script, [app.js](../app/interfaces/workbench/static/app.js) at
  765 lines, with 12 module-level mutable variables across its concerns. It polls three reads (stack
  5 s, runs 5 s, the selected run 2.5 s) and redraws each read's views.
- **Its acceptance** is `make demo`: real runs, the real Workbench and live terminals, every control
  pressed in headless Edge.

## Problem

The page shows everything D29 asks, but not in the order the operator needs it. Facts 12–16 rank the
least-used controls above the decision. Facts 7–11 say one fact in several places, and facts 17–21
name things by the system's words, not the operator's. Six defects (facts 1–6) make it wrong in
places, one of them on the research approval the operator is about to check (facts 2–3). Its code
redraws unchanged views on every poll (fact 6), and the redesign would take one mixed-concern script
past 1,000 lines — an estimate, about 400 lines of new views.

## Decision

### Design plan (`frontend-design`, first pass)

**Subject, audience, job:** A11.

**Principles**

1. **Attention first.** The rail's first group is *Needs you*; a run's first block is what you must
   decide. Routine controls sit one step away. Problems come to you as banners.
2. **One voice per role.** Engineer, architect and you each have one colour, used everywhere that role
   appears: the flow, the rows, the terminal headers, the history. Colour says *who*; words say *what state*.
3. **Evidence beside the decision.** The brief, the verdict, the failure, the plan and the change sit in
   or directly under the decision they support.
4. **Said once.** Each fact is shown once by default, and never deleted. The history keeps every entry's
   full text: one the decision already shows starts collapsed, marked *shown above*, and opens to its full
   text — once the run moves on, the history alone holds it.
5. **Explain in place.** Each field and answer says what it does, in the docs' words. A code gets a
   plain name.
6. **Quiet chrome.** Sections are flat, separated by space and rules. One raised element — the decision.
7. **The memorable element is the score.** The run's flow is drawn as a line of cues: done steps quiet,
   the current one ringed, your cues in amber. It is an ordered list, with `aria-current` on the step
   the run is at.

3:1, on both surface and raised)

face at up to 80 characters a line, with its line breaks kept; today it is set in monospace.

**What the run shows first, by state**

| run | first | then |
|---|---|---|
| waiting at an approval, a blocker or a spent budget | the decision, with its evidence | the plan (at a plan's approval), terminals collapsed, history |
| at the final gate | the decision, with the architect's verification | the change, terminals collapsed, history |
| failed | the decision, with the whole failure text | the failed role's terminal, history |
| working | the working role's terminal, open | the other terminal collapsed, history, the change once built |
| stopping | what it waits for, with Force terminate | terminals, history |
| closed | how it ended; what it kept, with Remove | the change if kept, history |

Whenever a run waits, its decision and answers are its first run controls in the DOM and the tab order,
and its own controls follow them (A3). No positive `tabindex`.

**Decisions** — titles and hints are the page's; the answers are the stop's own (A9)

| stop | title | evidence shown | its note is sent with |
|---|---|---|---|
| approval, phase `research` | Approve the research brief | the brief (`stop.feedback`) | Revise |
| approval, phase `plan` | Approve the plan | the architect's assessment, and the plan read from the change (fact 27) | Revise |
| approval, phase `build` — a flow with two builds (fact 30) | Approve the build | the architect's verification, and the change so far | Revise |
| blocker | The architect raised a blocker | the blocker | Guide |
| exhausted | The review rounds are used up | the last finding | Guide |
| failed | A step failed | the whole failure text, wrapped anywhere, with Copy — no path parsed out of it | — |
| final | Ready to merge | the last verification, the change, and a refused merge's reason | either Revise |

The workflow's hint stays each decision's subtitle, and is said once. The plan path is shown only from a
plan on (fact 3), relative to the worktree (fact 11). A refused answer is an alert beside what it
concerns (fact 21): at the note, which then takes focus, when the note is sent with that answer; beside
the answers otherwise.

**Copy**
- Stop reasons and statuses become words, for every ending the workflow or Temporal records: merged,
  discarded, stopped or done with the worktree and branch kept, refused with its reason, aborted,
  terminated.
- Durations through `Intl.DurationFormat` ("12 min"), past moments through `Intl.RelativeTimeFormat`

### Review against the brief (the design skill's check)

A generic plan for "redesign an operations dashboard" converges on six things. Each is changed here
because this brief is different:

- **Metric cards on top.** Dropped: the operator acts on no metric, and counts live in the group headers.
- **Green, yellow and red status badges everywhere.** Replaced by the role voices and one attention
  colour, so colour tells who is acting and whether it is you.
- **Identical cards for every section.** Replaced by one raised element, the decision; the rest is flat.
- **A generic stepper.** Replaced by the score, whose amber cues are the operator's own steps.
- **Metadata strings joined with dots.** Replaced by labelled facts, which explain themselves.
- **Walls of controls.** Replaced by a menu and banners.

### Web architecture

Evidence of need (D4): one 765-line script whose 12 shared mutable variables cross its concerns; views
redrawn on every poll (fact 6); two globals that tools reach into (fact 22); and ~400 more lines this
design adds. The change, with no framework, build step or dependency:

1. **Native ES modules, split by ownership:** a module for each concern that owns state or a lifecycle,
   not a file per heading. `app.js` only boots the route and the polls and wires the owners. The likely
   owners:
   - the API, and the common text-only UI helpers and formats;
   - the stack; the rail; the New run form, with the repository picker and the flows;
   - the selected run, whose history is a stateless view of its timeline;
   - the change reader, which keeps the parts read and their snapshot;
   - the terminals; the worktrees view.

   The terminals' owner keeps each terminal's `<details>`, xterm mount and socket. While the same run
   stays selected, no redraw of the run, its status or its history replaces them: the run view asks the
   owner to update a summary or open a terminal. Selecting another run may dispose and recreate them.

   No circular imports, and no `window.*` handle for the tools: the demo drives the page's DOM and URL as
   the operator does. xterm.js stays a classic script ahead of them.
2. **Redraw only what changed:** each view keeps the signature of what it last drew and skips an unchanged
   read — the pattern of `shownRepos` and `shownStop`, applied to every view — so a selection, focus and
   an open section survive an unchanged poll. Changed data may replace its section; nothing diffs to keep
   a selection across it. **Clocks apart:** each duration carries its `data-since`, and a small timer
   updates its text node, never its section.
3. **The URL fragment is the route** (A8). The rows become links (`<a href="#run=…">`), which gives
   keyboard, middle-click, reload, back and forward.
4. **`[hidden] { display: none !important }`** so that no layout rule can show what the page hid (fact 1).
5. **Native primitives, not widgets of our own** (fact 31):
   - the stack's actions in a `popover="auto"` panel opened by `popovertarget`, holding ordinary
     buttons — not an ARIA menu. A confirmation dismisses the popover (fact 33), so an action asked from
     it returns focus to the chip that opened it, and its result shows in the top bar's status line,
     outside the popover;
   - confirmations through `<dialog>.showModal()` and a `<form method="dialog">` (D6): Cancel focused
     first for a dangerous action, Escape cancels, and focus goes back to the control that asked, or to
     its popover's chip when the popover has gone; no focus trap of our own;
   - terminals and collapsed history entries as `<details>`/`<summary>`;
   - `aria-current="step"` on the flow's current step.
6. **The server's `STATIC` map lists each module, served as `text/javascript`:** an explicit allowlist,
   now tested.

### Premise / KISS gate

- **Owner.** The page, a presentation layer, owns this outcome. The data already has its owners, and the
  design reads them unchanged (A1).
- **Adds.** Module files and one allowlist entry each on the server; a native dialog in place of the
  browser's confirm, and a native popover for the stack's actions; the fragment route; a timer that
  updates clocks in place.
- **Removes.** The redraw of unchanged views; the always-visible stack buttons, lifecycle wall and
  worktree tool; the duplicated texts; the second repository picker on screen.
- **Given up, knowingly.** Controls that are always on screen: the stack's, two clicks away now (A5);
  an idle terminal, collapsed, and the browser's native confirm (both D6).

### Alternatives considered

- **Restyle and fix in place, one script.** The fewest files. It fixes facts 1–5, but leaves fact 6's
  redraws and the growth past 1,000 lines. D4 asks for the better structure when it is needed, and the
  evidence says it is.
- **A UI library (Preact, lit-html) or a bundler.** Rejected: a new vendored dependency or a build step,
  against architecture D29's plain page, for no need a module split does not meet.
- **Push (server-sent events) instead of polling.** Rejected: Temporal gives the page no event for a
  status change, so push would need a watcher per run on the server. The polls are cheap for one
  operator; the harm was the redraw.

## Required invariants

1. **Architecture D29 holds.** Every read and write goes through today's API; the page holds no run
   state. The fragment is the browser's, and names a run the server is still asked about.
2. **A stop's answers are the ones it publishes** (architecture D6):
   - the page never adds, hides or disables a published answer;
   - it never decides whether one is accepted — the workflow refuses, and the page shows the refusal;
   - labels, titles, hints and dialogs are the page's.
3. **Agent and run text reaches the DOM only as text**: `textContent` and text nodes, never markup from
   the API.
4. **Every capability stays reachable:**
   - each stack part's and the whole stack's start, stop and restart;
   - a start on a repository or a path, a flow and *skip approvals*;
   - every answer, *Stop run*, *Force terminate*, and the removal of what a run kept;
   - the worktrees view, older runs, and the change in parts with its snapshot check;
   - the terminals' typing and Esc, and the Temporal and Langfuse links.
5. **Every confirmation still asks before its action**, in words that keep the consequences
   architecture D31 and D32 state. `tools/demo.py` checks those words.
6. **The terminal stays 160×48**, the PTY's size (fact 26).
7. **Security as today:** the token in its header, never in a URL; the CSP unchanged; loopback only.
8. **Accessibility floor:**
   - every control reachable by keyboard, with a visible focus;
   - every control's boundary and the focus outline at 3:1 against their surface;
   - the result of the operator's own action in a polite `role="status"`, and a refusal as an alert
     beside its control; nothing polled — the rail, the history, the clocks, the stack's reading — is
     live;
   - reduced motion honoured;
   - colour never the only signal.
9. **Attention order is DOM order.** Whenever a run waits, its decision and answers come before *Stop
   run* and *Force terminate* in the DOM and the tab order; nothing uses a positive `tabindex`.
10. **The history keeps everything.** Collapsing an entry never removes its text, so the history alone
    reconstructs a run after it has moved on or closed.
11. **A terminal outlives its run's redraws.** While a run stays selected, only the terminals' owner
    replaces a terminal's DOM, xterm or socket; a change of the run's status never reconnects it, clears
    its buffer, or loses its scroll or selection.
